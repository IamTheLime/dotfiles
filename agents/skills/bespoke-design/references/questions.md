# Questions that shape the artifact

Use this as a coverage checklist, not a questionnaire to paste into chat.
Track topics as answered, delegated, irrelevant or still open in the brief.
Ask the next 1–3 questions with the largest effect on the outcome. A question
may be skipped when the answer is already clear; a required fact cannot be
silently treated as a preference.

## Purpose and people

- **Outcome:** What should someone remember, believe or do afterwards? What
  would make this successful?
- **Audience:** Who will see it? What do they know already, care about or doubt?
  Is there more than one audience to serve?
- **Context:** Where will they encounter it? What happens immediately before
  and after? Is the material public, internal, confidential or reusable?
- **Scope:** What is the topic, deadline and approximate length? What must be
  included, and what should stay out?
- **Decision:** Who chooses the direction and who needs to edit or approve it?

## Story and evidence

- **Message:** If the audience remembers one sentence, what should it be?
- **Structure:** Do you have an outline or should we propose one? Is this a
  pitch, explanation, report, lesson, portfolio or another kind of story?
- **Arc:** What needs establishing first? What is surprising? Which objection
  should be answered before the conclusion?
- **Material:** Which notes, documents, data, links or existing slides should
  supply the content? Which source wins if they disagree?
- **Editing:** May we rewrite, shorten and reorder the content? What wording
  must remain exact? What belongs in notes, appendices or supporting pages?
- **Evidence:** Which claims need citations, charts, demonstrations or quotes?
  Are any numbers provisional? What period, units and definitions apply?
- **Voice:** Conversational, precise, warm, formal, playful or something else?
  Which language, spelling convention and level of technical detail?
- **Ending:** What is the requested action, decision, next step or takeaway?

## Taste and identity

- **Feeling:** What should the first impression be? What should it never feel
  like? Offer 2–3 concrete contrasts if adjectives are too abstract.
- **References:** Which websites, decks, books, posters, spaces or brands do you
  like? For each, what specifically works: type, composition, imagery or mood?
- **Dislikes:** What looks generic, distracting or inappropriate to you? Are
  there existing examples you want to move away from?
- **Freedom:** Must this fit an existing brand, or can it establish a new one?
  What logos, colours, fonts, templates or accessibility rules are fixed?
- **Boldness:** Restrained, distinctive within familiar conventions, or highly
  experimental? Where can we take risks, and where must things stay familiar?
- **Typography:** Quiet and readable, expressive headlines, editorial contrast,
  technical precision, or another direction? Are licensed fonts available?
- **Colour and texture:** Light, dark or mixed? Any required or excluded colours?
  Flat, photographic, tactile, illustrated, geometric or something else?
- **Composition:** Spacious or information-rich? Ordered or asymmetric? Should
  imagery, a statement, a diagram or data lead the opening?
- **Signature:** Is there one motif or visual moment you want people to remember?
  Does it relate meaningfully to the subject?
- **Exploration:** Use the default 3 seeded concepts, a different number, or a
  previous direction? What must remain fixed during variations?

## Presentation branch

- **Use:** Presented live, read independently, or both? Does the reading version
  need extra explanation rather than merely exporting the live slides?
- **Time:** How long is the talk, including questions and demonstrations?
  Is slide count fixed, flexible or something we should recommend?
- **Room:** Projector, large display, video call or individual screens? What is
  the likely viewing distance, aspect ratio and lighting?
- **Format:** PowerPoint, Google Slides, Keynote-compatible file, PDF, browser
  presentation or several? Which must stay editable, and by whom?
- **Presenter support:** Speaker notes, timing cues, handout, appendix, backup
  slides, rehearsal outline or citations for later reading?
- **Pacing:** Mostly visuals with spoken explanation, denser evidence slides,
  gradual reveals or a mix? Where should the audience pause and reflect?
- **Participation:** Live demo, audience questions, polling, embedded media or
  interactive diagrams? What fallback is needed if these fail?
- **Navigation:** Linear talk or branching sections? For browser slides, do you
  need keyboard controls, fullscreen, touch navigation or a progress indicator?
- **Portability:** Must it work offline, on another computer or without custom
  fonts? Is a static PDF or printable version required?

## Website branch

- **Visitors:** What task brings people here? What is the primary action and
  where should it lead?
- **Scope:** Landing page, several pages, portfolio, story, dashboard or app?
  What pages or sections are required, and which can be combined?
- **Devices:** Mobile first, desktop first or equal? Any device, browser or
  connection constraints? Does it need offline behaviour?
- **Behaviour:** Which links, forms, filters, search, downloads or interactions
  need to work? Is any data live, editable or illustrative?
- **Integration:** Existing repository, stack, design system, CMS, analytics,
  form destination or services to retain? Is there a backend already?
- **States:** What should happen during loading, errors, empty results and
  successful submission? Are accounts or restricted areas in scope?
- **Discovery:** Search metadata, social preview, localization or deep links?
- **Ownership:** Who updates content after handover, and how?
- **Launch:** Local files, preview link or deployment? What domain and hosting
  destination should be used, and has publication been requested?

## Assets, motion and practical limits

- **Existing assets:** Which logos, photos, illustrations, screenshots, video
  or diagrams are available? Are they approved for this audience and use?
- **New assets:** Should we create illustrations, find licensed imagery, use
  supplied media or keep the design typographic? Any likeness or brand limits?
- **Data visuals:** What comparison or change must each chart communicate?
  Do editable charts and source data need to accompany the output?
- **Motion:** None, subtle emphasis or a central part of the experience? Which
  moments benefit from it? Must it survive export to slides or PDF?
- **Media:** Would a generated loop or transition help explain something?
  Are autoplay, sound, looping and download size acceptable for this context?
- **Access:** Any particular reading, vision, language, keyboard or motion
  requirements? Need captions, transcripts or meaningful image descriptions?
- **Resources:** What is the time or paid-media budget? Are we limited to tools
  and assets already available? What is more important if time runs short?
- **Handover:** Required filenames, folders, source files, export types, preview
  links and instructions? Who must be able to reopen and edit the result?

## Questions after a preview

- Which direction feels closest, and what exactly makes it work?
- Which parts should we preserve, remove or borrow from another direction?
- What feels wrong: the story, mood, density, typography, images or composition?
- Is it too safe, too theatrical, too busy or too sparse for this audience?
- Should we refine this direction, vary one feature or reroll the open choices?
- Is there a requirement we have not discussed that would change the result?

## Ready to build

Before full production, account for the audience, outcome, source material,
delivery format, chosen direction, hard constraints and remaining unknowns.
Present a short brief for correction. Do not add a separate approval gate when
the user has already selected a direction and authorised the build.
